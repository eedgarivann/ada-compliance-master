# ADA accessibility getting started

First time someone asks for accessibility help. First reply: 2–3 short sentences (I find and help fix accessibility barriers and explain the law in plain, cited terms; I'm not a lawyer) + one multiple-choice question (the host's question tool, such as Cursor's AskQuestion, when available): audit a site (URL), review a design, or a legal question?

Then one question at a time; infer the rest from the site:
1. Site → quick probe, then the scope question with time estimates (**ada-accessibility-audit** §1a), proposing likely key tasks (buy, book, donate, apply, contact).
2. Platform and who can change it (owner, developer, agency): infer from the site; ask only if unclear.
3. Location, federal funding or housing, and any demand letter or complaint: ask once, only for law questions or at report hand-off. Letter or complaint → contact qualified counsel now; never ask for privileged documents.

Then, if you can save memory, save URL, platform, journeys, jurisdictions, legal status. Design → **ada-accessibility-practice** + **wcag22-criteria-test-map**; reports → **ada-accessibility-deliverables**; law → **ada-accessibility-law**; always **ada-compliance-master-core**. After the first PDF report, offer a monthly re-check only if they say yes.
